# Asteroid brief (as of 10 Oct 2026)

Researched 10 Oct 2026 by a Haiku 5.5 agent. Pages marked *(snippet)* were only seen in search results because the full page would not open. Content writers: check NASA's CNEOS and planetary-defence pages before publishing any flagged number.

## 1. Asteroid 2024 YR4: the Moon question is closed
- **Discovery:** found in late December 2024 by the ATLAS telescope in Chile ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_YR4) *(snippet)*).
- **Earth:** the Earth-impact chance peaked at 3.1% in mid-February 2025 and fell to 0.004% (about 1 in 25,000) by 24 Feb 2025 ([Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/world-news/2025/02/25/nasa-gives-all-clear-asteroid-threaten-earth/)). NASA says it "poses no significant impact risk to Earth in 2032 and beyond" ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/2024-yr4)).
- **Size:** about 60 m (±7 m), roughly a 15-storey building ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12351); [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/2024-yr4)).
- **Moon:** the odds were about 4.3% in 2025. Webb observed it on 18 and 26 Feb 2026, and NASA's 5 Mar 2026 update says experts "have ruled out any chance of 2024 YR4 impacting the Moon on Dec. 22, 2032" ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/2024-yr4)). It will pass about 21,200 km above the lunar surface ([Sky & Telescope](https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-news/lunar-impact-from-asteroid-2024-yr4-ruled-out/)), or about 22,900 km from the Moon's centre ([AAS Nova](https://aasnova.org/2026/05/20/asteroid-2024-yr4-wont-hit-the-moon-new-jwst-observations-show/)).
- **If it had hit:** a crater about 1 km wide, up to 10^8 kg (about 100,000 tonnes) of rock thrown up, and extra tiny debris hazard for satellites ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11217)).
- **Deflection:** a study judged deflection missions "impractical" and gave disruption launch windows of late 2029 to 2032 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12351)). No deflection mission was planned. NASA says the community "continues to monitor" it ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/2024-yr4)).

**For the app's conundrum:** frame it as the real decision of 2025, when the Moon odds were about 1 in 25, and reveal that the world chose to watch with Webb, and the March 2026 measurements showed a miss by about 21,000 km.

## 2. Apophis
- About 375 m wide and about 20 million tonnes ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Planetary_Defence/Apophis)).
- On 13 April 2029 it passes less than 32,000 km from Earth's surface. For a short time it is closer than geostationary satellites, and it is visible to the naked eye from parts of Europe, Africa and Asia ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Planetary_Defence/Apophis)).
- **OSIRIS-APEX (NASA):** flew past Earth in September 2025 at about 3,438 km. As of 30 Sep 2026 it was about 89 million km from Earth, and it will study Apophis after 2029 ([APEX](https://apex.asteroidmission.org/)). The FY2027 White House request proposed ending it, but House appropriators' May 2026 bill would restore $20 million ([AAS](https://aas.org/posts/news/2026/05/house-fy27-cjs-report-nasa-and-nsf-details) *(snippet)*).
- **Ramses (ESA):** an €81.2 million build contract was signed 10 Feb 2026. Launch is planned for spring 2028, and it will rendezvous with Apophis before the April 2029 flyby ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Planetary_Defence/ESA_awards_contracts_for_Ramses_mission_to_Apophis)).

## 3. DART and Hera
- DART hit Dimorphos on 26 Sep 2022 at 23:14 UTC ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02077v1)). Its orbit period dropped by 33.0 ± 1.0 minutes, versus about 7 minutes expected without debris kicking it further ([Carnegie Science](https://carnegiescience.edu/orbital-period-change-dimorphos-due-dart-kinetic-impact)).
- ESA's Hera launched 7 Oct 2024. It is due to arrive at Didymos in November 2026, a month earlier than planned, with two CubeSats ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Hera/ESA_s_Hera_targets_early_arrival_at_Didymos_asteroids)). No report that it has arrived yet.

## 4. Sample returns
- **Bennu (OSIRIS-REx):** the sample contains 14 of the 20 amino acids life uses to build proteins, all five DNA/RNA letters, and unusually high ammonia. The findings do not show life itself ([MPE](https://www.mpe.mpg.de/8051538/news20250129)). It also holds salts left by an ancient brine that evaporated ([Smithsonian](https://dashboard.si.edu/es/newsdesk/releases/traces-ancient-brine-discovered-asteroid-bennu-contain-minerals-crucial-life) *(snippet)*).
- **Bennu's 2182 odds:** a 2021 study gave about 1 in 2,700 for 24 Sep 2182. Odds through 2300 are about 1 in 1,750 ([Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/152167/osiris-rex-got-to-know-bennu-really-well-apparently-theres-now-a-1-in-1750-chance-that-itll-hit-earth-by-2300) *(snippet)*).
- **Ryugu (Hayabusa2, Japan):** 5.4 g returned in 2020. One sample held a drop of carbonated water with salt ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2022-09-asteroid-clues-life-earth.html)).

## 5. Chelyabinsk 2013 and Tunguska 1908
- **Chelyabinsk:** an object about 18 m wide and about 11,000 tons exploded over Russia on 15 Feb 2013, releasing about 440 kilotons ([NASA JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/additional-details-on-the-large-feb-15-fireball-over-russia/)). It injured more than 1,000 people, mostly from broken window glass ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/exploding-meteor-over-russia-injures-more-than-1-100-1.1312154) *(snippet)*). **App note:** keep this gentle; say windows broke and people got cuts, nobody died.
- **Tunguska:** in 1908 it flattened about 2,000 km² of Siberian forest with no crater, with energy estimated at up to 15 megatons ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Tunguska-event) *(snippet)*).
- **How often (averages):** about 1 m, a few times a year; about 19 m, every 60–80 years; about 50 m, every 200–300 years; about 1 km, about every 500,000 years ([Catalina Sky Survey FAQ](https://catalina.lpl.arizona.edu/faq/how-often-do-asteroids-strike-earth) *(snippet)*).

## 6. Planetary defence options (for the kid's vote)
- **Kinetic impactor:** crash a spacecraft into it. DART did this ([Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/articles/asteroid-deflection-techniques-to-save-the-earth)).
- **Gravity tractor:** a parked spacecraft's gravity slowly tugs it off course. It needs months or years of warning and may not work on rocks over 500 m ([Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/articles/asteroid-deflection-techniques-to-save-the-earth)).
- **Laser ablation:** lasers vaporise the surface, and the puffs of gas push the rock ([Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/articles/asteroid-deflection-techniques-to-save-the-earth)).
- **Ion beam shepherd:** an ion engine's exhaust gently pushes it. It needs a second thruster to keep the spacecraft from being pushed away ([Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/articles/asteroid-deflection-techniques-to-save-the-earth)).
- **Nuclear blast:** a last resort. It could shatter the rock into several large pieces, so one impact becomes several ([Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/articles/asteroid-deflection-techniques-to-save-the-earth)).
- No source found for a "paint it" option.

## 7. How we find them
- **Count:** NASA reported 39,123 known near-Earth asteroids on 3 Dec 2025, 873 of them over 1 km, with about 50 of those still unfound ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids-comets-meteors/near-earth-asteroids)). The Minor Planet Center lists 42,443 near-Earth objects discovered to date ([MPC](https://www.minorplanetcenter.net/)). The MPC handles asteroid positions, names and orbits.
- **NEO Surveyor:** an infrared space telescope that finds dark asteroids. Launch is no earlier than September 2027 ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/neo-surveyor/)).
- **Vera Rubin Observatory:** its first images (23 June 2025) found 2,104 new asteroids, 7 of them near-Earth, in about 10 hours. Its survey is expected to find millions more ([Rubin](https://rubinobservatory.org/news/first-imagery-rubin)).

## 8. Psyche and Lucy
- **Psyche:** about 280 km wide and probably the metal core of an early planet. It flew past Mars on 15 May 2026 and arrives in summer 2029 ([NASA JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-psyche-mission-aces-mars-flyby-targets-metal-rich-asteroid/)). Mining is not part of the mission.
- **Lucy:** visits Trojan asteroids (groups that share Jupiter's orbit). The first flyby is Eurybates and its moon Queta on 11 Aug 2027, then Polymele, Leucus, Orus, and Patroclus–Menoetius on 3 Mar 2033 ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lucy/)).
