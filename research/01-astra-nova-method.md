# Astra Nova and Synthesis: research brief

Researched 10 Oct 2026 by a Haiku 5.5 agent. Facts below were checked by fetching the school, ClassDojo, contest, and Synthesis pages. Third-party claims are flagged.

## 1. Conundrums

**What they are.** Roughly one-minute ethical-dilemma videos made by Astra Nova and distributed through ClassDojo ([ClassDojo library](https://ideas.classdojo.com/b/conundrums/)). ClassDojo lists 30 titles; the [contest site](https://conundrumscontest.com/) says there are 63 as of May 2026. Episodes are also on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/c/astranovaschool) (listed, not fetched).

**Structure.** Each video poses a dilemma with a conflict at its center, offers 3–5 options, has no right answer, and ends on a question that "leads right into a discussion" ([contest site](https://conundrumscontest.com/); [ClassDojo](https://ideas.classdojo.com/i/the-moonshot-conundrum/)). A downloadable worksheet helps viewers "organize your thinking" ([ClassDojo](https://ideas.classdojo.com/i/the-treasure-conundrum/)).

**How kids respond.** Public sources describe classroom discussion, not a submission loop. Reasoning is captured in admissions: applicants answer one Conundrum on a 30-second to 2-minute video, because the school wants "to hear how your child reasons through the question" ([Astra Nova](https://www.astranova.org/)). Contest entrants write their own. No source found saying public responses are shared among viewers.

**Ages.** The contest says Conundrums "appeal to a wide range of ages" and advises avoiding violence or suffering ([contest site](https://conundrumscontest.com/)). Astra Nova's own tracks are middle school 11–14 and high school 14–18 ([Astra Nova](https://www.astranova.org/)).

**Examples**
- **Treasure:** An ancient treasure is found using a translated map. Who deserves the greatest share? ([ClassDojo](https://ideas.classdojo.com/i/the-treasure-conundrum/))
- **Moonshot:** A country wants to "make life better" by concentrating money on a few goals. How should it spend? ([ClassDojo](https://ideas.classdojo.com/i/the-moonshot-conundrum/))
- **Ferry (#22):** Two growing cities are linked by a slow, beloved ferry with lengthening queues. Which upgrade is best? Summary from a search excerpt, not verified on the [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I9qlNUlcrxk).
- **Arcade:** A rigged claw machine is treated as having two sides. This is a critic's description, quoted in [Mother Jones](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2025/03/elon-musk-schools/).

Other titles (NASA, Dragon, Bird, Photo, Arctic, Pizza, Halloween) have no verified summaries.

## 2. Core principles

- **Rebuilt yearly from first principles.** Astra Nova "designs its school anew each year from first principles," aiming for "genuine curiosity, problem-solving, and intellectual courage" ([astranova.org](https://www.astranova.org/)).
- **Problem-solving over tools.** Ad Astra centered "problem-solving and critical thinking" ([Interesting Engineering, 2020](https://interestingengineering.com/culture/astra-nova-school-following-elon-musks-ad-astra-school-experiment-leads-the-future-of-education)). Per The Hustle, Synthesis lets kids "work out how the systems operate" in simulations ([The Hustle, 2024](https://thehustle.co/meet-synthesis-the-edtech-startup-scaling-elon-musks-ad-astra-school)).
- **Constructive disagreement.** Conundrums build "constructive disagreement about big ideas" ([astranova.org](https://www.astranova.org/)).
- **Collaboration as the north star.** Dahn: "The north star is how does this help us create a generation or cultivate a generation of super collaborators?" ([NAIS New View EDU ep. 21](https://www.nais.org/learn/nais-podcasts/new-view-edu/episode-21-how-to-create-a-generation-of-super-collaborators/), ~34:26).
- **Reflection.** Dahn calls reflection "a really hard thing to do" (same episode). Synthesis students "analyze what worked, what failed, and why" ([Synthesis Teams](https://www.synthesis.com/teams)).
- **Mistakes expected; kids as decision-makers.** "Mistakes are expected" ([Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor)). Games give "real experience being leaders, decision-makers, and critical thinkers" ([Synthesis](https://www.synthesis.com/)).
- **No grades or tests.** "No lectures or tests" ([Synthesis Teams](https://www.synthesis.com/teams)). The Hustle says Synthesis gives no grades. The no-grades claim for Astra Nova comes only from a review site.

Dahn's own statements are sparse; most principles above are school copy or paraphrase.

## 3. Synthesis

**Team games (ages 8–14, live weekly).** Two formats, each offered twice a week: 60-minute "fast-paced team simulations" and 45-minute discussions where kids "debate ideas, weigh trade-offs" ([Synthesis Teams](https://www.synthesis.com/teams)). Each session ends with a coach-guided reflection, and any session can be repeated. Current price: $95/month, no contract.

**Resource trade-offs.** The clearest example is a game where students "explore space, stake territory and gather resources." One 9-year-old weighs a riskier network against a better payoff. Afterward "the class reconvenes for group analysis," and rules sometimes change mid-game ([Deseret News, 2023](https://www.deseret.com/2023/3/13/23633154/synthesis-spacex-elon-musk-public-schools-vouchers/)). Resource and scoring mechanics aren't published. Facilitators give little instruction, and students work out rules and scoring themselves ([Interesting Engineering, 2021](https://interestingengineering.com/culture/learning-through-play-how-synthesis-plans-to-bring-the-ad-astra-astra-nova-model-to-the-entire-world)).

**Synthesis Tutor (ages 5–11, K-5 math, AI-based).** Adaptive, with "micro-assessments" in every lesson, and kids advance on mastery. For children under 7, the Tutor reads all text and answer choices aloud at an adjustable speed ([Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor)). Listed family price: $29/month for up to 7 kids, with a 7-day trial.

## 4. Design rules for a tablet (dad and son)

1. **Predict, then reveal the split.** The child picks first, then the screen shows how others chose. The contest site says the best Conundrums split the audience fairly evenly, so write dilemmas that way.
2. **Vote, then explain.** After the pick, record a 20-second voice "because..." The explanation is the product.
3. **No right-answer screen.** Replace "Correct!" with "What would change your mind?"
4. **Two-player roles.** Give each player different clues, like the three contributors in Treasure, so dad and son have to talk.
5. **Fixed debrief.** End every activity with "What worked? What didn't? Why?"
6. **Shared trade-off game.** A 5–10 minute round with a shared budget or territory, one risky option, and one mid-round rule change.
7. **Read aloud, retry freely.** Narrate everything, let any word be tapped to hear it, allow unlimited retries, and show no scores.
8. **Gentle dilemmas.** Avoid violence and suffering, per the contest guidance.

## Caveats

- **Conflicting facts.** Astra Nova's age range is 11–14 on its site, 10–14 in Mother Jones, and 9–14 in a 2020 article. Synthesis Teams is $95/month now but $180/month in older coverage. Mother Jones lists Tutor at $180/year; the site lists $29/month.
- **Controversy.** Mother Jones (March 2025) reports critics of the Conundrum framing and quotes Synthesis co-founder Chrisman Frank calling most teachers "glorified babysitters." Dahn says Astra Nova and Synthesis are independent of Musk.
- **Not found.** How public Conundrum responses are reviewed, and any official rationale for the Tutor's 5–11 design.
