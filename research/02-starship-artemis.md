## Starship

**Current vehicle: Block 3 ("V3"), first flown 22 May 2026** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket)); [Basenor](https://www.basenor.com/pages/spacex-starship-tracker))
- Stacked height about 124.4 m (408 ft); diameter 9 m; stainless steel; both stages burn liquid methane and liquid oxygen ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket))).
- Super Heavy booster: 33 Raptor engines. Ship: 6 Raptors (3 sea-level, 3 vacuum) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket))).
- Payload to low Earth orbit: about 100 t. Wikipedia's thrust figures conflict, so none are used here ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket))).

**Cutaway labels for a kid:** Raptor engines; methane and oxygen tanks (booster about 3,650 t, ship about 1,600 t propellant); payload bay (Starlink satellites); header tanks at the payload bay tip; four flaps; about 18,000 heat-shield tiles rated to about 1,400 °C; grid fins; the vented interstage used for hot staging ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket))).

**Ship catch:** none yet. Flight 15 is provisionally no earlier than 19 Oct 2026 as the first ship-catch attempt. This is unconfirmed and comes from secondary reporting ([TeslaNorth](https://teslanorth.com/2026/09/15/spacex-flight-15-ship-catch-odds/)).

**Step by step** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket)) unless noted)
1. **Liftoff:** all 33 booster engines ignite.
2. **Max-Q** (peak air pressure on the rocket): no source reviewed covers this step.
3. **Hot staging** (about 141 s, about 64 km): the booster cuts 28 engines and the ship lights its engines before separating.
4. **Boostback:** the booster flips and relights engines to fly back toward the landing site.
5. **Landing burn and tower catch:** the booster uses grid fins to steer, then slows with its engines. Its arms are caught by the tower's "chopsticks." V3 test flights 12–14 chose ocean landings instead.
6. **Ship coast:** the ship's six engines push it to orbital speed, then shut down. Flight 14 was cut from a planned 10 hours to 3 after an engine failure ([WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/national/2026-09-28/spacexs-starship-launches-on-first-orbital-mission-from-texas)).
7. **Re-entry "belly flop":** the ship falls at about 60° to the ground, slowed by its heat shield and flaps.
8. **Landing flip and burn:** the ship's engines turn it upright shortly before landing.

**Mars and Moon**
- SpaceX moved Mars to the "back burner" in February 2026 and now targets an uncrewed Moon landing "as early as 2027" ([Payload](https://payloadspace.com/spacex-delays-mars-plans-in-favor-of-the-moon/)). No Mars Starship found on any manifest.
- Orbital propellant transfer, needed for the Moon lander, is not reported done. In May 2026 The Register said SpaceX had not yet shown orbit or fuel transfer ([Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/05/01/artemis_iii_aims_for_late/)).

**Sites:** Starbase is at Boca Chica, Texas ([WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/national/2026-09-28/spacexs-starship-launches-on-first-orbital-mission-from-texas)). Flights 12–14 launched from Orbital Launch Pad 2 ([Basenor](https://www.basenor.com/pages/spacex-starship-tracker)). Pad 1 is under reconstruction. Florida's LC-39A and SLC-37 are under construction ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket))).

## Artemis and Moon base

- **Artemis I** (launched 16 Nov 2022, splashed down 11 Dec 2022 off San Diego): uncrewed. Orion went 268,563 miles from Earth ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-i/)).
- **Artemis II** (launched 1 Apr 2026, splashed down 10 Apr 2026): crewed lunar flyby, 9 days 1 hour 32 minutes. Crew: Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch (NASA), and Jeremy Hansen (CSA) ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-ii/)).
- **Artemis III:** NASA's 9 June 2026 release says it is a 2027 Earth-orbit test, not a landing. Orion docks with a Blue Origin lander test article for about 2 days, then a Starship test article for about 1 day. Crew: Randy Bresnik, Luca Parmitano (ESA), Andre Douglas, Frank Rubio; backup Bob Hines ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-marches-toward-artemis-iii-mission-in-2027-names-crew-members/)). The Register reports Isaacman's testimony placing it in late 2027 ([Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/05/01/artemis_iii_aims_for_late/)).
- **Artemis IV:** first crewed south-pole landing, planned for 2028 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-marches-toward-artemis-iii-mission-in-2027-names-crew-members/)).
- **Gateway:** paused on 24 Mar 2026. NASA is shifting its hardware and people to the surface ([Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/24/goodbye_lunar_gateway_nasa_ditches/)).
- **Moon Base:** three phases. Phase 1 (to 2029) is robotic. Moon Base I is Blue Origin's Mk1 lander "Endurance," which slipped to early 2027 after New Glenn exploded on the pad ([SpaceFlightNow](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/06/30/blue-origin-outlines-return-to-flight-logistics-for-its-new-glenn-rockets/)). Phase 2 begins in 2029 with short-term habitation ([EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/space/moon-base-update-nasa-unveils-next-steps/)). NASA's line: "We're going back to the Moon. This time, to stay." ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase/))
- **Lunar terrain vehicles:** Astrolab and Lunar Outpost were selected in late May 2026 ([Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2026/05/27/nation-world-news/nasa-buys-two-cars-for-its-astronauts-to-drive-on-the-moon/)).
- **Why the south pole:** Shackleton Crater's rim is sunlit more than 90% of the year, while its interior stays permanently shadowed. Those shadowed areas are thought to hold water ice ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/shackleton-craters-illuminated-rim-shadowed-interior/)).
- **Habitats:** NASA has not chosen a design. The inflatable-plus-3D-printed-regolith concept is from ESA and Foster + Partners ([Foster](https://www.fosterandpartners.com/projects/lunar-habitation)). Lava-tube bases are a university project ([Mines](https://www.mines.edu/capstoneseniordesign/project/moon-rocks-lunar-habitat)).

**China**
- In February 2026 the China Manned Space Agency said it still targets a crewed landing "by around 2030." It reported Mengzhou escape tests, Lanyue landing and takeoff tests, and a Long March 10 low-altitude flight ([Xinhua via Henan gov](https://english.henan.gov.cn/2026/02-27/3329140.html)).
- **ILRS:** Wu Weiren said in April 2024 that a "basic station" would be built by 2035 in the south-pole region, expanded by 2045 ([Leonard David](https://www.leonarddavid.com/china-moon-base-plans-evolve/)). No 2026 revision found.
- **Chang'e-7** targeted August 2026 for the south pole. Its lander goes to Shackleton's rim and a hopper enters a shadowed crater ([CIS Lunar](https://www.cislunarspace.cn/en/space-news/2026/08/2026-08-01-chang-e-7-launch-international-payloads/)). Launch not confirmed.

## 10 wow facts for a 6-year-old

1. Starship is about 124 m (408 ft) tall ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket))).
2. Its booster lights 33 engines at once at liftoff ([WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/national/2026-09-28/spacexs-starship-launches-on-first-orbital-mission-from-texas)).
3. Giant metal arms caught a rocket booster in mid-air in October 2024 ([SciAm](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/spacexs-starship-soars-in-6th-test-flight-but-skips-booster-catch/)).
4. Starship's skin has about 18,000 tiles that handle about 1,400 °C ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket))).
5. Starship falls belly-first like a skydiver, then flips upright to land ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_(rocket))).
6. On 28 Sept 2026, Starship reached orbit for the first time, at about 17,500 mph ([WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/national/2026-09-28/spacexs-starship-launches-on-first-orbital-mission-from-texas)).
7. That flight released 26 Starlink V3 satellites, three with cameras to watch the heat shield ([WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/national/2026-09-28/spacexs-starship-launches-on-first-orbital-mission-from-texas)).
8. Artemis I's Orion flew 268,563 miles from Earth ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-i/)).
9. In April 2026, four astronauts flew around the Moon and back in 9 days ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-ii/)).
10. Shackleton Crater's rim gets sunlight most of the year, but its floor is always dark and may hold ice ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/shackleton-craters-illuminated-rim-shadowed-interior/)).

- Flight 15 and Chang'e-7 launch status are unconfirmed as of 10 Oct 2026. Blue Moon Mk1 is now targeted for early 2027.
- Several Starship details (flights 12–14 specifics, Flight 15 timing, flight 9–10 booster outcomes) come from blogs and prediction markets. Verify before publishing.
